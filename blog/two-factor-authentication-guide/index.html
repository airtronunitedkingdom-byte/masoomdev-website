Two-Factor Authentication: A Beginner's Guide

Add a second layer of protection to important accounts.

What you need to know

Two-factor authentication requires another proof of identity beyond your password, reducing risk when passwords are exposed.

Authenticator apps commonly generate time-based codes, while security keys can offer stronger phishing resistance.

Save recovery codes somewhere secure and separate from the device used for authentication.

Never share one-time codes with someone who calls or messages claiming to be support staff.

Enable two-factor authentication first on your email account, then other accounts that could be used to reset passwords.

Quick checklist

  • Identify your goal and the required output.
  • Keep an original copy before making changes.
  • Verify the result on the device or service where it will be used.
