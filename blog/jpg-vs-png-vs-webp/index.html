JPG vs PNG vs WebP: Which Format Should You Use?

Choose image formats by content, transparency and compatibility.

What you need to know

JPEG is a practical choice for photographs because its lossy compression often produces smaller files than PNG.

PNG preserves precise edges and transparency, which makes it useful for logos, interface captures and graphics containing text.

WebP supports both lossy and lossless compression and transparency, so it can be a good web-delivery format.

Avoid repeatedly saving a JPEG after edits: each lossy re-encoding may introduce more artifacts. Keep a lossless working copy.

Test your image in the actual page or marketplace where it will appear, since platform processing can affect the result.

Quick checklist

  • Identify your goal and the required output.
  • Keep an original copy before making changes.
  • Verify the result on the device or service where it will be used.
